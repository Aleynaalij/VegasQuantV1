## Mandatory selection and parlay review — adopted October 16, 2026

Owner-approved lessons from SEA–DEN on October 15 apply to every future qualitative selection and parlay, including halftime and entertainment releases. These are editorial decision gates, not a validated probability model or permission to bypass existing release controls.

1. **Opportunity before proximity.** Each leg needs a sourced role/workload case, credible remaining opportunities, and an explicit failure path. “Only needs a few more yards/catches” is not sufficient. At halftime, record the observed score/clock, carries, targets and production; review routes/snaps and recent-drive usage where verified. Missing participation, availability or live data stays visibly unknown. Do not invent these inputs or extrapolate first-half production by simply doubling it.
2. **Sustained drives before scoreboard assumptions.** A lead alone does not establish rushing opportunity; a deficit alone does not establish productive passing. Review possession availability, offensive success, pressure/protection, stalled drives, rotation and opponent adjustments. Separate penalty-assisted yards, turnover short fields and isolated explosive plays from repeatable offensive production.
3. **Role before box-score totals.** Evaluate target/carry persistence and quality of opportunities rather than choosing the closest threshold. Compare receptions and yardage only using supported usage evidence. Secondary injuries do not automatically translate into receiver production when protection or pressure limits the offense.
4. **No forced leg count.** Select defensible legs first, then determine ticket length. A requested five-leg or 10–12-leg format is an aim, never a reason to add a weak selection. Publish fewer supported legs or no parlay, and explain the shortfall. Merely disclosing a material unsupported assumption does not make it acceptable.
5. **Review the whole ticket.** Before publishing a bundle, document how its legs interact under plausible leading, trailing and stalled-offense scenarios. Identify shared failure paths and conflicting workload assumptions, including rushing versus passing and teammates competing for opportunities. Do not multiply marginal probabilities as though same-game legs were independent. No combined probability, odds, EV or payout without defensible supporting evidence.
6. **Separate decision quality from results.** Review narrow winners as critically as narrow losers. Preserve the time-of-decision evidence and original targets; never retrofit a better-looking ticket after the outcome. Distinguish supported inference from observed facts and identify what was knowable before selection.

For every proposed leg, retain a compact case covering: verified role/opportunity evidence; remaining-opportunity case for live selections; matchup/game-script assumptions; why it could lose; and material unknowns with timestamped sources. If the evidence cannot support the selection, withhold it rather than fill a slot. Retain this review in supported analysis/details structures without rewriting immutable publications.

For postgame audits, deduplicate identical player/game/market/direction/threshold selections across reposts, Pick of the Day and bundles when reporting unique-leg performance. Report ticket outcomes separately; distinct thresholds and markets remain distinct but correlated selections. Never equate leg hit rate with parlay success or profitability. No ROI/profit claim without verified ticket price, stake and settlement evidence.

These rules govern research/editorial review. They do not add live-data coverage, retrain v0.3, validate SHADOW_ONLY output, change historical picks/results, or authorize wagers.
